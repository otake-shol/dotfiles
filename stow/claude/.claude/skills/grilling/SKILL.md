---
name: grilling
description: Interview the user relentlessly about a plan or design. Use when the user wants to stress-test a plan before building, or uses any 'grill' trigger phrases.
---

Interview me relentlessly about every aspect of this plan until we reach a shared understanding. Walk down each branch of the design tree, resolving dependencies between decisions one-by-one. For each question, provide your recommended answer.

Ask the questions one at a time, waiting for feedback on each question before continuing. Asking multiple questions at once is bewildering.

If a question can be answered by exploring the codebase, explore the codebase instead.

For consequential assumptions, separate established facts, hypotheses, and preferences. Ask what observable evidence would change the user's mind, and record that condition before looking only for supporting evidence. Investigate counterevidence as well; missing evidence is not proof that an assumption is false. Do not demand empirical proof for a value preference.

Present the strongest reasonable opposing view before testing a decision, preserving its actual premises and tradeoffs. Keep the one-question-at-a-time format. Do not reopen settled decisions without relevant new evidence, and do not treat agreement or confidence as factual verification. Record unresolved uncertainty and agreed conditions for revisiting provisional decisions instead of questioning indefinitely.
